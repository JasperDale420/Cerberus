# Critique: Cerberus Regime Labeling Pipeline

## 1. Are the labels usable as-is?

**Verdict is conditional and answerable in 30 minutes.** The decision splits cleanly on one fact — how the 69 parquets are consumed — so the responsible answer is to do the audit before making operational moves, not to gate operational moves on a layout-based prior.

What is *established* by the evidence pack, independent of consumption:

1. **The per-symbol vol column is severely miscalibrated against any plausible interpretation of "market volatility regime."** Median symbol spends 94.3% of days HIGH-or-SHOCK; only 3 of 68 names reach LOW on ≥5% of days; aggregate 0.32% LOW vs. 67.6% HIGH/SHOCK against a SPY-truth distribution of ~9%/~80%/~18%/~2%. Fact about the column, not a claim about its use.
2. **Train and live use different estimators** (SMA + absolute thresholds vs. Hurst + EWMA z) with no documented invariant tying them and no standard report comparing them on common dates.
3. **The Risk axis is silently absent from training data and silently present in live gating.** Strategies whose `activation:` keys on `RISK_ON` condition on a signal autoresearch never observed.
4. **Reported 81.9% accuracy is in-sample** — same SPY periods tune and score.

What is *not* yet established:

- Whether autoresearch joins per-symbol parquets, joins only the SPY parquet, or treats `vol_regime` as a feature versus a filter.
- Whether the "ground truth proportions" used for the 81.9% were generated by an independent rule or by the labeler's own threshold logic. Without this the accuracy is *at least* in-sample; whether also tautological is open.
- Whether 98.6% HIGH on TSLA still carries useful conditional information — multi-year ~3.6 days/yr in NORMAL is rare, but rare and uninformative are not the same; mutual information against forward returns settles it.

**For the labels themselves:** already-shipped autoresearch findings that condition on `vol_regime` for non-SPY symbols should be marked *provisional* in the results store (a metadata flag, not a quarantine) until the audit and an OOS spot-check land. Reversible, cheap, proportionate.

## 2. Highest-leverage fix

**The highest-leverage *action* is the audit (P0 below) — choosing a fix before knowing how the labels are consumed is a guess. Conditional on confirming per-symbol joining, the highest-leverage *redesign* is option (a) plus a co-published market-state column, but only after held-out OOS ground truth exists to choose between candidate calibrations.**

- **(a) Per-symbol percentile thresholds.** Directly fixes the calibration evidence: a defensive ETF and a meme stock both reach LOW at the same percentile of their own history — what "low vol for this asset" should mean. The cost — that "HIGH" no longer means the same physical vol across symbols — is real, mitigated by *also* publishing a market-state column with documented, validated thresholds.
- **(b) Replace Labeler A with Labeler B.** Train/live convergence is genuinely valuable, but only if B is validated. Hurst on daily bars over a short window is itself noisy and regime-dependent. Adopting B without OOS validation against multiple asset classes trades one unvalidated estimator for another, and removes the only diagnostic we have (A-vs-B divergence). Convergence is the destination, not the next step.
- **(c) Add Risk axis to training data.** Necessary regardless, but does not fix the vol-axis miscalibration. Independent; ship in parallel.
- **(d) The actual highest-leverage object is the *ground truth*, not the labeler.** Both A and B are unscored against held-out OOS multi-asset truth. Until that exists, "which labeler is better" cannot be answered, only debated. Building defensible truth (P1) unlocks every other decision.

On absolute thresholds: applied to SPY they are not a category error — SPY's distribution is the implicit reference for the 8/20/50 numbers. Applied to TSLA they are wrong *for TSLA's distribution*, not wrong as a concept. The architecture is to keep absolute thresholds where they make sense (a market-level column, calibrated to its own benchmark) and use percentile thresholds for the per-symbol column.

## 3. Hidden assumptions that might invalidate the critique

1. **Consumption pattern (load-bearing).** If autoresearch loads only the SPY parquet, every per-symbol miscalibration finding becomes "delete unused artifacts and document the contract." If it joins per-symbol, calibration findings translate into training distortion. The 69-parquet layout is consistent with either; conventions and dead artifacts produce the same shape. Resolve by reading the autoresearch driver and `src/agent/`.
2. **Filter mode vs. feature mode.** Distortion magnitude differs by an order or more. A boolean filter inherits miscalibration directly; a tree model fed `vol_regime` as one feature can attenuate noise — but can also exploit the constant-feature × symbol-id interaction as a symbol-class proxy. Audit should record both.
3. **81.9% accuracy construction.** If SPY ground truth was independently labeled by humans, in-sample tuning is the only critique. If generated by the same threshold scheme being scored, the metric measures self-consistency. Evidence pack is silent; verify before promoting circularity from suspicion to claim.
4. **Information content of rare bins.** 98.6% in HIGH leaves ~3.6 days/yr in other bins. Whether that residual carries actionable conditional return information is empirical (mutual information of `vol_regime` with forward 1-day return on TSLA, vs. permutation null). Measure before declaring "uninformative."
5. **Risk-axis 58% baseline.** "58% accuracy" is meaningful only relative to class balance and a constant-classifier baseline. If RISK_ON is ~80% of days, 58% is *below* always-predict-RISK_ON. If ~50%, 58% is real but weak. Recompute before treating as the punchline.
6. **OOS regime exists.** Hand-labeling 4-6 periods (TSLA, JNJ, sector ETF, commodity ETF, plus held-out SPY) is feasible in a day. Without it every labeler choice is vibes.

## 4. Concrete dangers

1. **Symbol-class skew presented as regime conditioning** (conditional on per-symbol joining). With most non-SPY symbols ~constant in HIGH/SHOCK, `groupby(vol_regime)` for those names approximates a partition by symbol identity. Reports labeled "edge in HIGH regime" describe unconditional-on-time-state behavior of a symbol class.
2. **HIGH-regime claims on non-SPY names are nearly equivalent to unconditional claims.** Promoting on "outperforms in HIGH on TSLA" is, mechanically, promoting on TSLA's unconditional return for ~98.6% of the sample.
3. **LOW-regime strategies cannot be evaluated on 65 of 68 symbols.** Mean reversion, theta selling, carry — edges concentrating in low realized vol — have no LOW observations for almost the entire universe. Looks like null edge; is missing data.
4. **Train/live divergence is silent.** Detectable only if a comparison report exists. None does. Worth fixing regardless of which estimator is chosen.
5. **Risk-axis gating on a near-noise classifier.** Whether "weak" or "useless" depends on baseline; either way, autoresearch never trained against it, so the live conditional edge is unmeasured.
6. **Reported accuracy may be self-consistent rather than informative.** Verify before relying on 81.9% for any uncertainty estimate or promotion.

## Prioritized fix list

1. **P0 — 30-minute consumption audit.** Read the autoresearch driver, `src/agent/`, and config resolution. Record (i) per-symbol vs. SPY-only join, (ii) feature vs. filter use, (iii) every `activation:` block keyed on `vol_regime` or `risk_state`. Gates everything below.
2. **P0 — Verify the 81.9% ground truth construction.** Read `optimized_params.json`, the labeling script, any SPY truth file. Confirms whether truth is independent or threshold-derived.
3. **P0 conditional — Mark non-SPY regime-conditioned findings provisional.** Metadata flag in results store; do *not* block live promotions on a flag alone. Fires only if (1) confirms per-symbol joining; lifted when P1 spot-checks land.
4. **P1 — Multi-asset OOS ground truth.** Hand-label 4-6 held-out periods (JNJ, TSLA, sector ETF, commodity ETF, SPY periods *not* in the original 8). Score A *and* B on it. Until this exists, picking a labeler is vibes.
5. **P1 — Information-content tests on per-symbol `vol_regime`.** Mutual information of bin assignment with forward 1-day return by symbol, vs. permutation null. Distinguishes informative-but-rare from truly uninformative; prevents throwing away rare-but-real signal.
6. **P1 — Risk axis: ship a real classifier or stop emitting it.** Recompute 58% against actual class balance and constant-classifier baseline. Either re-derive the proxy to beat baseline by a meaningful margin on hand-labeled stress windows (Mar 2020, Jan 2022, SVB, Aug 2024 yen carry), or remove the axis and have `RISK_ON`-gated strategies treat it as constant. Either beats current.
7. **P2 — Redesign Labeler A: market-state column + per-symbol z-score column.** Co-published, documented thresholds; estimator selected by P1 ground-truth performance, not preference. Same estimator in train and live. No silent alias of old `vol_regime` — force consumers to choose.
8. **P2 — Document the regime contract.** `REGIME_CONTRACT.md` next to the labeler: axes, estimators, threshold derivation, hysteresis, train/live invariant, validation sources. Methodology drift is the symptom; absent contract is the disease.
9. **P3 — Aggregate-distribution CI checks.** Per labeler emit, fail the build if (i) the market-state column disagrees across symbols on any common date, (ii) >50% of universe in a single bin >80% of days for the per-symbol column, (iii) train and live disagree on the market-state column for any common date in the last 30 days. Universe-aggregate only — per-symbol forced distributions would false-positive on genuinely-quiet ETFs.
